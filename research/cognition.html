Cognition

The common thread is stimulus choice. If you want to know what cortex does with a concept, the experiment has to contain the concept being used rather than being tested for.

The conventional way to ask whether two cognitive domains share machinery is to run a small experiment per domain and check whether the activated regions coincide. Each experiment carries its own task, its own stimuli and its own confounds, so “overlap” ends up being partly a statement about how the experiments were built rather than about the brain.

The alternative is a stimulus rich enough to contain all of it at once, and a model whose coordinates can be read — which is what the work under Bayesian is for.

Number, space and time2022–2026

A theory from 2003 holds that the brain measures how many, how far and how long with one shared system. Behaviour supports it; imaging has disagreed with itself. Read out of story listening — one experiment, one stimulus stream, three concepts each on their own axis — the three maps overlap, in the intraparietal sulcus and the inferior frontal gyrus, bilaterally, across listeners. Nineteen listeners later the picture is a distributed temporal, parietal and frontal network in which some regions are sharply selective for one quantity and others answer to all three.

The video is the viewer switching those three channels on and off over the group map. The viewer itself does this on click.

Birdsong in state space2017–2019

The same instinct applied to a smaller animal, earlier. A zebra finch song is normally treated as acoustics: a spectrogram, an inventory of syllables. The alternative is to treat the recording as one observable of a dynamical system and reconstruct the state space behind it by delay embedding, taking the delay from the first minimum of the mutual information and checking the whole procedure against systems whose answers are already known. The video is one song’s reconstructed state space, turning.
