Cognition

An area, not a project

Brain viewer Number, space and time Birdsong

The common thread is stimulus choice. If you want to know what cortex does with a concept, the experiment has to contain the concept being used rather than being tested for.

Why it is a category

The conventional way to ask whether two cognitive domains share machinery is to run a small experiment per domain and check whether the activated regions coincide. Each experiment carries its own task, its own stimuli and its own confounds, so “overlap” ends up being partly a statement about how the experiments were built rather than about the brain.

The alternative is a stimulus rich enough to contain all of it at once. Someone telling a story counts things, places them and times them continuously, in one stream, in one currency — words. A single encoding model fit to that gives every voxel a weight for every feature, and the maps for different concepts become directly comparable because they came from one experiment. The price is that the features have to be disentangled first, which is what the work under Bayesian is for.

Number, space and time

A theory from 2003 holds that the brain measures how many, how far and how long with one shared system. Behaviour supports it; imaging has disagreed with itself. Read out of story listening, the three maps overlap — in the intraparietal sulcus and the inferior frontal gyrus, bilaterally, across listeners. Nineteen listeners later, the picture is a distributed temporal, parietal and frontal network in which some regions are sharply selective for one quantity and others answer to all three. The full tour is here, and the maps are open in the viewer.

Song
