Birdsong in state space

2017–2019, with Eve Armstrong and Vijay Balasubramanian at the University of Pennsylvania

APS abstract Song Attractor

  1. In short
  2. The question

  4. Where it got to
  5. Outputs

In short

I treated a zebra finch’s song as one measurement of the physical system that produces it, and tried to rebuild the shape of that system’s motion from the recording alone.

The question

Zebra finch song is usually described as sound: a spectrogram and an inventory of syllables. But the song is produced by a physical system with its own dynamics, and a recording is one measurement of that system. The question was whether that system’s geometry could be recovered from the recording, and what it would show that the acoustic description leaves out.

The approach

A result from dynamical systems theory, Takens’ theorem, says that the shape of a system’s trajectory can be rebuilt from a single measured channel, by treating delayed copies of that channel as extra coordinates. In practice: take the waveform, choose a delay, and plot each moment against its values one and two delays earlier. The delay matters (figure below). I set it with the standard criterion, the first delay at which a copy shares the least information with the original, and estimated alongside it how many coordinates were needed and whether paths that start close together stay close.

A reconstruction like this draws a picture even from noise, so I ran the same procedure on two systems whose answers are already known, the Lorenz and Rössler systems, and kept those next to the song reconstructions as controls. The corpus was ten zebra finch songs, segmented so that the geometry could be compared syllable by syllable. A short animation shows one song’s reconstruction turning in three dimensions.

Where it got to

The work, with Eve Armstrong and Vijay Balasubramanian, was presented at the APS March Meeting in 2019, and it stopped there when I moved to Berkeley. The published claim is the abstract, and this page describes the method only. The same question runs through my later work on word embeddings and on movement at home: whether a signal holds structure that its usual description leaves out.

The work appeared under Xiao Zeng.
