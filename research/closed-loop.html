Closed loop

A closed loop is a system that measures the thing it is also acting on. Deep brain stimulation is tuned in a clinic room over an afternoon, for a disease that behaves differently at home over a day.

That is a harder object to study than either half alone, because the signal you are reading is partly a consequence of what you did last, and because the interesting behaviour only appears on timescales and in settings a laboratory visit cannot reach. The work here is about earning the right to close the loop at all: getting synchronised implanted, wearable and video recordings out of somebody’s living room, and then showing that behaviour predicts neural activity well enough that stimulation can be driven from it.

Parkinson’s, measured at home2021–2026

Participants implanted with bidirectional neurostimulators were recorded at home while performing a standardised motor battery: local field potentials from the subthalamic nucleus, electrocorticography from precentral and postcentral gyri, bilateral wrist accelerometry and video-based pose estimation. With behaviour recorded at that resolution, encoding models can predict neural power from behaviour — and which features you use matters, since the full acceleration spectrogram and task labels beat coarse summaries like mean acceleration by a wide margin.

The video is Figure 1 of the paper, filling in: the same session under under-, preferred- and over-stimulation, advancing together on one clock. With Simon Little (UCSF) and Jeffrey Herron (UW).
