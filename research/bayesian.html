Bayesian

What I mean by this heading is narrower than it sounds. Not Bayesian as a school — Bayesian as the discipline of asking what a fitted parameter is actually entitled to mean.

A model can predict well and still support no inference at all. If the features it is fit on are correlated enough, many different parameter settings produce identical predictions, and the fitted weights are not a measurement of anything — they are one arbitrary point in a set the data cannot distinguish between. Prediction accuracy will not warn you about this. Nothing in the fit will.

So the work here is about the conditions under which a fitted model is allowed to be read: identifiability, sparsity, and priors chosen so that the basis a thing is expressed in is one where the coordinates mean something separately.

Sparse concept atoms2021–2025

Word embeddings predict cortical responses well and explain them badly: they carry more latent semantic factors than they have dimensions, so the factors sit in superposition and a voxel’s weights are not identifiable. Replacing the dense basis with a thousand sparse, non-negative concept atoms — one interpretable direction each — leaves prediction accuracy unchanged and makes the weights readable atom by atom. The video is what the models are fit to: measured responses while one story plays.
