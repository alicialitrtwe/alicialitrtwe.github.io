Learning Sparse Semantic-Cortical Atoms for Multisubject Naturalistic fMRI Encoding

Alicia Zeng

NeurIPS 2026, oral · Sydney

Written for this page from the paper’s abstract; the paper itself is on OpenReview, and it is presented as an oral at NeurIPS 2026 in Sydney, with a poster in Poster Session 2.

The short version

Encoding models built on language-model features predict cortical responses to natural speech well, and they remain hard to read. Three things stand in the way: selectivity is not identifiable in a correlated, high-dimensional feature space; the organisation of cortex stays implicit in voxelwise weight maps; and correspondence across people is handled only after the model is fit. This paper puts all three inside one model.

A multisubject coupled sparse autoencoder reconstructs the language-model features and predicts each subject’s brain responses at the same time. What it learns is a sparse basis of semantic-cortical atoms: each atom is a direction in meaning and, by construction, the pattern of cortical response that goes with it, in every subject at once. Semantic structure, cortical organisation and cross-subject correspondence are therefore explicit in the fitted object rather than recovered from it afterwards.

  • 88%of a matched dense-feature ridge model’s prediction performance, retained
  • 50atoms in the learned basis
  • k = 5atoms active per time point

In naturalistic story-listening fMRI, that basis keeps 88% of the prediction performance of the dense model with fifty atoms and five active per TR. Against matched two-stage baselines, which learn the basis first and fit the brain second, joint training gives the strongest overall trade-off between prediction and interpretability. The atoms are semantically coherent; they give more reproducible cross-subject maps than standard post hoc encoding-model analyses; they align with large-scale cortical structure from the Yeo 7 networks and from Neurosynth; and they transfer better than ridge to held-out subjects when data are scarce.

It is the successor to the 2025 paper, which unpacked the embedding into sparse concept atoms first and fit the encoding model second. Here the two steps are one, and the subjects are modelled together rather than one at a time.

Abstract

Naturalistic language encoding models based on large language model features can predict cortical responses well, but their learned representations remain difficult to interpret. Three challenges are central: semantic selectivity is fundamentally non-identifiable in high-dimensional correlated feature spaces, cortical organization remains implicit in voxelwise weight maps, and cross-subject structure is usually addressed only after model fitting. We introduce a multisubject coupled sparse autoencoder that jointly reconstructs language-model features and predicts subject-specific brain responses, learning a sparse basis of semantic-cortical atoms that makes semantic structure, cortical organization, and cross-subject correspondence explicit within a single model. In naturalistic story-listening fMRI, the model retains 88% of matched dense-feature ridge brain prediction performance using 50 atoms and k = 5 active atoms per TR. Relative to matched two-stage baselines, joint training yields the strongest overall tradeoff between prediction and interpretability. The learned atoms are semantically coherent and interpretable, recover more reproducible cross-subject brain maps than standard post hoc encoding-model analyses, align with large-scale cortical structure from Yeo 7 and Neurosynth, and transfer better than ridge to held-out subjects in the low-data regime. This framework provides a more interpretable basis for cognitive neuroscience analyses of semantic-to-brain encoding by linking semantic features to corresponding cortical response patterns across subjects.
